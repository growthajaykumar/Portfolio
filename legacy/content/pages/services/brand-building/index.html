Overview

Brand work gets dismissed as the soft part of marketing, usually by people who have never watched a well-positioned competitor win a deal at price parity. In practice, positioning is the highest-leverage document a company owns: it decides what you build, who you sell to, what you charge and what you say.

The process is structured and fast. Interviews with your customers — especially the ones who nearly bought and didn’t. A competitive language audit. Then two workshops with the founding team to force the trade-offs positioning actually requires, because a position that excludes nobody is not a position.

The output is a messaging architecture the whole team can use: one sentence anyone can repeat, the proof underneath it, and the words to use for each audience and channel.

Approach

How I think about brand building.

Positioning is a series of refusals. The hardest part of every workshop is getting a team to say out loud who they are not for. Until that happens the messaging stays hedged, and hedged messaging is invisible.

I also treat brand as measurable, which annoys people on both sides of that argument. Branded search volume, direct traffic, share of voice, win rate against named competitors and the cost of a click on your own category terms all move when positioning improves. Slowly — but they move.

The stakes differ by market. In fashion, brand is the product; discounting is usually a positioning failure wearing a pricing costume. In Web3, brand is the difference between a community and a queue of mercenaries. In real estate, it is what lets you defend a price per square foot the project next door cannot.

Questions
Isn’t brand building only for big companies?

The opposite. Large companies can buy attention until the message lands. Small companies cannot, which makes clarity their cheapest advantage — and positioning costs a fraction of the media budget it saves.

How do you measure brand work?

Branded search volume, direct traffic, sales-cycle length, win rate against named competitors, and cost per click on category terms. We baseline all of them before starting so the comparison is honest.

Do you design logos?

I direct the work rather than draw it. Identity design runs with a trusted design partner while I own the brief and the strategic line, which is what keeps the visuals accountable to the positioning.

How long does a positioning engagement take?

Typically four to six weeks: two for research and interviews, two for workshops and the messaging architecture, two for rollout across site, deck and core collateral.

What if my team disagrees on positioning?

That is normal, and it is what the workshops are for. Disagreement about who you serve and what you refuse to be is a strategy problem, not a communication one — and far cheaper to surface in a workshop than in a quarter of flat pipeline.
